Build a production-quality interactive website called:

**THE MCU CHRONICLE**  
*One universe. Countless lives. Every consequence.*

The website must explain:

- How the MCU begins and evolves.
- The life story and defining decisions of important characters.
- How every MCU feature film connects.
- The major locations, factions, artifacts, conflicts, and consequences.
- The best first-time viewing order.
- Alternate chronological, character, saga, and catch-up paths.

The result must feel like an authored, explorable story world first and an archive second.

Primary audience:

- New viewers who need orientation without spoilers.
- Existing fans who want relationships, chronology, and character arcs.
- Returning viewers who want a curated rewatch path.

The first five minutes must be understandable without prior MCU knowledge. Deeper information must be progressively disclosed.

## 3. Why previous versions failed

Treat these as known causes:

1. Too much content was requested before visual quality was proven.
2. “WebGL,” “3D,” “particles,” and “parallax” were mistaken for art direction.
3. Procedural toruses, glowing lines, and generic space backgrounds created a technology demo.
4. A route checklist encouraged cards, pills, grids, filters, and dashboards.
5. “Award-winning” was stated but not enforced through screenshot comparison.
6. Dark backgrounds, red light, dust, and bloom became default styling.
7. One abstract hero object was forced to carry every chapter.
8. The agent completed content breadth before one exceptional viewport existed.
9. The design had no repeatable world-building grammar.
10. The interaction revealed decoration rather than meaningful information.
11. Mobile was treated as a crop of desktop.
12. Weak assets were hidden behind effects.

Version 6 fixes the incentives by making the **tableau**, **graphic frame**, and **connection layer** the primary deliverables.

When requirements compete:

1. Static composition.
2. Asset and subject quality.
3. Primary/connection-layer relationship.
4. Graphic framing.
5. Typography.
6. Scene continuity.
7. Interaction response.
8. Mobile recomposition.
9. Accessibility and fallback.
10. Performance.
11. Content depth.
12. Feature quantity.

The build is locked behind four gates:

### Gate A — Tableau Gate

Build only the opening desktop tableau and its held state.

Pass only when:

- The primary frame looks exceptional without motion.
- The hidden layer reveals meaning, not a color filter.
- Subject, type, and frame have a clear visual hierarchy.
- The image remains strong in grayscale and at 12 px blur.
- No dashboard, card grid, generic cosmic field, or stock sci-fi object appears.

### Gate B — Window Gate

Build one complete transition from Prologue to Origin.

Pass only when:

- Scroll works forward and backward.
- The Continuity Line motivates the new frame.
- The stencil-window reveal is spatially understandable.
- Holding switches layers smoothly.
- Releasing restores the primary layer without a pop.

### Gate C — World Gate

Build all eight opening tableaux.

Pass only when:

- Every tableau feels different but belongs to one system.
- Each scene has one focal subject.
- The Continuity Line remains traceable.
- Copy has a readable hold.
- The final scene naturally becomes navigation.

### Gate D — Mobile Gate

Recompose at `390 × 844` and `360 × 800`.

Pass only when:

- Tap-to-hold works without blocking scroll.
- The subject crop is intentional.
- The hidden layer remains legible.
- Frames become simpler rather than merely smaller.
- The experience still feels authored.

Archive scale and bulk content may begin only after these gates pass.

## 6. Core creative concept

### Name

**The Living Chronicle**

### Premise

The visitor enters a future archive reconstructing the MCU from fragments of decisions, relationships, places, and consequences.

This is not a database floating in space. It is an editorial machine that opens cinematic windows into the story.

### Recurring visual device: the Continuity Line

Use one precise red line that begins in the loader and survives the entire site.

It can:

- Divide a frame.
- Become a mask edge.
- Trace an event connection.
- Underline a character decision.
- Form a timeline.
- Fold into a film number.
- Split during Civil War.
- disappear during Infinity.
- Resume in Legacy.
- Branch into five navigation paths at the end.

Rules:

- Default width: `1 px`.
- Emphasized width: `2–3 px`.
- It must never become a glowing neon spaghetti network.
- No more than five branches are visible at once.
- It always communicates structure, state, or causality.

### Two information layers

Every major tableau has two authored states:

#### Layer 1 — Story

Shows:

- The cinematic image.
- The emotional headline.
- Date or phase metadata.
- One short, spoiler-safe statement.

#### Layer 2 — Connections

Revealed by holding:

- Why the moment matters.
- What decision caused it.
- Which characters, films, factions, or events it connects.
- Optional spoiler-safe or full-spoiler information.
- Diagrammatic annotations anchored to actual parts of the tableau.

This is not a modal. It is the same world seen through a different analytical lens.

### Signature interaction

Press and hold:

- Begins after `180 ms`.
- Slows current tableau motion to `20%`.
- Slides or flips graphic frames into the connection composition.
- Reveals the secondary scene through stencil windows.
- Changes the Continuity Line from narrative path to causal diagram.
- Clarifies labels and relationship anchors.
- Reverses smoothly on release.

Touch:

- Touch and hold activates after `260 ms`.
- Movement beyond `12 px` cancels the hold and preserves page scrolling.
- A deliberate tap on `EXPLORE CONNECTIONS` toggles the state for accessibility.

Keyboard:

- Space holds when the experience is focused.
- Enter toggles the connection layer.
- Escape returns to Story.

### Signature moment

In the Infinity tableau, the Continuity Line reaches a central decision and stops. Half the scene is removed through a hard-edged stencil, not a generic fade. The interface remains still for one reading beat. Holding reveals the absent relationships as precise empty outlines. In Legacy, three new lines grow from incomplete endpoints; the missing content is not restored.

## 7. Visual position and design grammar

### Design sliders

- Quiet `3 / 10` → Expressive `7 / 10`.
- Editorial `7 / 10` → Spatial `8 / 10`.
- Precise `8 / 10` → Organic `3 / 10`.
- Timeless `6 / 10` → Trend-aware `4 / 10`.
- Restrained `4 / 10` → Immersive `8 / 10`.

Emotional promise:

> A precise future archive repeatedly interrupted by impossible cinematic windows.

Primary visual tension:

> Clean off-white editorial space versus deep, atmospheric story worlds.

Dominant compositional rule:

> Every viewport has one oversized subject, one assertive frame, one short statement, and one continuous line.

Signature effect:

> Hold-to-reveal a second story dimension through a reconfigured stencil window.

### Palette

Interface colors:

```css
--paper: #F2F0EA;
--paper-warm: #E7E2D8;
--ink: #0A0B0D;
--ink-soft: #26282C;
--meta: #77756F;
--line: rgba(10, 11, 13, 0.18);
--signal-red: #B51F28;
--legacy-gold: #B59A52;
```

Scene-only light:

```css
--origin-warm: #C78450;
--assemble-cold: #8EA2AC;
--cosmic-blue: #607E91;
--fracture-cyan: #8DAEB6;
```

Rules:

- The interface is approximately 72% off-white, 20% ink, 6% red, 2% gold.
- Purple is not a default accent.
- Dark tableaux may exist inside frames; the whole site must not become permanently black.
- Red indicates causality, active state, or unresolved tension.
- Gold is reserved for Legacy and completed watch paths.
- Avoid rainbow Infinity Stone UI colors.
- Never glow body text, frame borders, and buttons.

### Typography

Use an original open or licensed variable sans—not KPR’s Whyte.

Recommended:

```css
--font-display: "Roboto Flex", "Arial Narrow", Arial, sans-serif;
--font-body: "Inter", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif;
--font-editorial: "Source Serif 4", Georgia, serif;
--font-mono: "IBM Plex Mono", Consolas, monospace;
```

The display face must support weight and width variation.

Desktop scale:

```css
--hero: clamp(82px, 9.4vw, 148px);
--tableau: clamp(58px, 6.4vw, 104px);
--page-title: clamp(72px, 10vw, 156px);
--index-title: clamp(38px, 4vw, 64px);
--body-large: clamp(20px, 1.6vw, 28px);
--body: clamp(16px, 1.08vw, 19px);
--meta: 11px;
--nav: 13px;
```

Headline rules:

- Maximum three lines.
- Maximum 11 words.
- Line height `0.84–0.93`.
- Tracking `-0.055em` at large sizes.
- Width axis may move from 115% to 82% during transitions.
- Weight may move from 420 to 680.
- Type can overlap the frame edge but not the subject’s face or key silhouette.
- Do not animate individual letters except the loader glyph.

Metadata:

- Mono.
- `10–12 px`.
- Tracking `0.06–0.11em`.
- Sentence case for explanatory text.
- Technical codes may be uppercase.

### Grid

Desktop `1440 × 900`:

- 24 columns.
- Outer margin: `32 px`.
- Gutter: `12 px`.
- Header baseline: `36 px`.
- Footer/control baseline: `864 px`.
- Major frames snap to columns but may break the grid by at most two columns.

Laptop:

- 16 columns.
- Outer margin: `24 px`.
- Gutter: `10 px`.

Mobile:

- 6 columns.
- Outer margin: `18–20 px`.
- Gutter: `8 px`.

### Frame language

Use five frame families:

1. **Slit** — a narrow vertical or horizontal window that expands.
2. **Offset rectangle** — one clean frame displaced from the grid.
3. **Split frame** — two opposing windows separated by the Continuity Line.
4. **Triptych** — three unequal panels showing simultaneous locations or times.
5. **Cut frame** — an L-shaped or stepped aperture used for the final navigation.

Frame rules:

- Square corners or maximum `2 px` radius.
- Border: `1 px` ink at `18–38%`.
- No rounded cards.
- No glassmorphism.
- No drop shadow around every frame.
- A scene uses one dominant frame family.
- Adjacent scenes cannot use identical frame geometry.
- Empty frame regions are allowed and should create tension.

### Texture and material

Interface:

- Soft paper grain: `1.5–2.5%` opacity.
- Rare registration marks.
- One faint technical grid only when the connection layer is active.
- No simultaneous grid, stars, dust, scanlines, and film scratches.

Tableaux:

- Cinematic CG or high-quality 2.5D.
- Matte blacks, weathered metal, stone, fabric, architecture, haze.
- Lighting belongs to the scene.
- Bloom is below `0.35`.
- Chromatic shift is off except a brief Multiverse transition.

## 8. Asset production system

The coding agent may not claim high visual quality using default primitives.

### Required asset package per major tableau

Create:

   - Optimized GLB, or
   - Transparent rendered image sequence, or

2. One background plate.
3. One middle-ground layer.
4. One foreground occluder.
5. One connection-layer alternative.
6. One mask map.
7. One depth map when 2.5D parallax is used.
8. One static fallback composition.

Minimum spatial separation:

```text
Background: parallax factor 0.12
Middle ground: 0.35
Hero subject: 0.62
Foreground: 1.00
Interface: fixed
```

### Asset-first workflow

Before production coding:

1. Write `ASSET_BRIEF.md`.
2. Create three style frames for the opening tableau.
3. Test all three in grayscale.
4. Select one visual language.
5. Produce the opening Story and Connections states.
6. Pass the Tableau Gate.
7. Produce remaining assets.

Never produce all assets before the first style frame is approved.

### Fixed illustration style: Archival Graphic Realism

All generated website imagery must use one locked style called:

**ARCHIVAL GRAPHIC REALISM**

Style statement:

> Cinematic semi-realistic 3D illustration translated through premium futuristic editorial print design: realistic adult anatomy, sculptural silhouettes, tactile matte materials, precise directional lighting, deep atmospheric space, controlled black/ivory/signal-red grading, hard graphic crops, subtle registration and halftone texture, and restrained narrative symbolism.

This style is fixed across:

- The loader.
- All eight opening tableaux.
- Story and Connections layers.
- Film archive previews.
- Film detail tableaux.
- Character portraits.
- Character Life Line illustrations.
- Timeline event imagery.
- Watch-order hero frames.
- Static and mobile fallbacks.

Do not change illustration style by page, phase, character, or scene. Change subject, location, lighting temperature, frame shape, crop, and narrative emphasis—not the underlying rendering language.

### Fixed visual DNA

Use these values as a binding style contract:

| Attribute | Fixed direction |
|---|---|
| Realism | `70%` realistic form, `30%` graphic stylization |
| Base medium | High-end 3D/CGI illustration with a 2D editorial finish |
| Anatomy | Realistic adult proportions; grounded weight; restrained poses |
| Silhouette | Large readable masses with one asymmetrical identifying feature |
| Surface | Matte, weathered, tactile; no plastic toy gloss |
| Edges | Crisp focal edges; softer atmospheric edges at depth |
| Shadows | Deep charcoal, not crushed pure black everywhere |
| Color | Desaturated cinematic base; red used structurally; gold only for Legacy |
| Lighting | One motivated key, restrained rim, minimal fill |
| Lens | `35–65 mm` equivalent; avoid extreme wide-angle distortion |
| Depth | Four separable planes suitable for parallax |
| Texture | Fine paper grain, faint registration offset, selective halftone |
| Finish | Premium concept-art tableau inside a precise editorial system |
| Emotion | Serious, mythic, intelligent, human; never childish or camp |

### Non-negotiable consistency rules

- Use the same image-generation model family and version for every production asset.
- Use the same global style prefix verbatim.
- Use the same negative prompt verbatim.
- Use the selected style-anchor image as a reference whenever the tool supports image references.
- Record the model, version, seed, prompt, dimensions, reference images, and revision number.
- A scene-specific prompt may add content but may not rewrite the fixed style.
- Do not mix photorealistic, comic-cartoon, anime, painterly, clay, paper-cut, low-poly, and glossy 3D treatments.
- Do not use a different artist imitation for different scenes.
- Do not add text, logos, UI, labels, frame borders, or Continuity Lines inside generated artwork.
- All typography, lines, annotations, frames, and marks are rendered by HTML/SVG/WebGL.
- Do not generate a flattened “finished webpage screenshot” and use it as the website.
- Generate reusable layers with clean edges and intentional overlap.

### Camera and crop rules for generated assets

Characters:

- Full figure or three-quarter figure: `50–65 mm`.
- Paired characters: `45–55 mm`.
- Groups: `35–45 mm`.
- Close material details: `70–90 mm`.

Environments:

- `28–40 mm`.
- Correct verticals unless a purposeful low-angle monument shot is specified.
- Horizon and vanishing point must match the intended desktop frame.

Avoid:

- Lenses below `24 mm`.
- Facial close-ups wider than `50 mm`.
- Random Dutch angles.
- Cropping hands, heads, or feet accidentally.
- Centering the subject unless the shot bible explicitly requires it.

### Lighting rules for generated assets

Every prompt specifies:

```text
KEY: direction, softness, temperature, intensity role.
RIM: side and ratio.
FILL: ratio.
PRACTICAL: source visible or implied.
BACKGROUND: value and separation.
```

Default ratio:

```text
Key 1.00
Rim 0.32
Fill 0.12
Ambient 0.10
```

The subject must remain readable with bloom removed.

### Color-grade lock

Across all assets:

- Overall saturation: restrained, approximately `55–72%` of a typical cinematic render.
- Blacks lift slightly toward charcoal.
- Highlights remain warm ivory rather than blue-white.
- Skin, if visible, stays natural and is not tinted purple or red.
- Signal red occupies at most 4% of an illustration.
- Gold appears only in Legacy, watch completion, or inherited paths.
- Scene color may shift temperature, but the black/ivory/red relationship remains.

Create and reuse one LUT or CSS/WebGL grade:

```text
public/assets/style/archival-graphic-realism.cube
```

If a LUT cannot be produced, record equivalent grade values in `style-anchor.json`.

### Texture lock

Apply texture after image generation or during compositing:

- Paper grain scale: `0.7–1.3 px` at 1440 px display width.
- Grain opacity: `1.5–2.5%`.
- Halftone only in shadow transitions or analytical overlays.
- Halftone dot diameter: `1–2 px` at display scale.
- Registration shift: maximum `0.7 px`.
- Do not cover the entire image in heavy noise.
- Do not use fake distressed paper tears unless the scene specifically requires damage.

### Candidate and revision policy

For each layer:

1. Generate four candidates.
2. Reject obvious anatomy, style, and composition failures immediately.
3. Compare the two strongest at the actual frame crop.
4. Select one.
5. Use edit/inpaint/outpaint for targeted correction.
6. Maximum three full regenerations before revisiting the prompt or composition.
7. Do not composite unrelated candidate parts unless lighting and perspective match.
8. Record the approved revision.

Do not choose the most detailed image automatically. Choose the image with the strongest silhouette, crop, negative space, and consistency.

```text
4  Foreground occluders
```

Readable text remains semantic HTML.

The Story and Connections scenes may both exist in WebGL, but only pixels inside the active stencil windows are rendered from the hidden scene.

### Stencil-window principle

Use WebGL stencil buffering or an equivalent mask/render-target system:

1. Render the hidden scene only where the active window mask writes to stencil.
2. Render the primary scene outside or beneath the mask.
3. Move the frame and mask with the same normalized reveal progress.
4. Keep DOM annotations positioned from the same frame configuration.
5. Discard pixels outside the hidden window.

Fallback:

- Use a clipped CSS/image layer with `clip-path`.
- Preserve identical Story/Connections information.
- Do not leave a blank Canvas.

## 13. Motion direction

Motion character:

> Graphic, dimensional, precise, energetic at transitions, elegant at rest.

The site must not drift constantly. Each tableau needs a real reading hold.

### Timing tokens

```ts
const motion = {
  response: 0.12,
  micro: 0.22,
  frameShift: 0.58,
  textIn: 0.68,
  textOut: 0.36,
  windowReveal: 0.72,
  tableauEnter: 1.10,
  tableauExit: 0.82,
  cameraSettle: 0.90,
  routeTransition: 1.05,
  easeEditorial: [0.16, 1, 0.3, 1],
  easeGraphic: [0.76, 0, 0.24, 1],
  easeExit: [0.7, 0, 0.84, 0],
  easeMechanical: [0.65, 0, 0.35, 1]
};
```

### Five-phase motion grammar

Every major transition contains:

1. **Signal** — the Continuity Line or frame edge indicates change.
2. **Release** — current copy and framing make room.
3. **Transformation** — stencil, camera, and subject change together.
4. **Registration** — the new frame snaps optically to the grid.
5. **Hold** — motion reduces so the visitor can read.

The transformation is not complete until registration and hold occur.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calibration → Origin | `0.058–0.074` | `0.074–0.088` | `0.088–0.112` | `0.112–0.132` | `0.132–0.154` |

- Supporting copy enters after the frame registers.
- Do not fade all text identically.
- Do not animate every word.
- Never sacrifice reading time for continuous movement.

### Frame motion

- Frame edges move linearly enough to feel graphic.
- A frame may overshoot by `4–8 px`, never by 10% of its size.
- Masks and visible borders must remain locked within `1 px`.
- Use subpixel transforms but snap the final frame to whole CSS pixels.
- Border opacity may peak during transformation and settle afterward.
- No elastic bounce on large frames.

### Parallax

Pointer:

- Background maximum displacement: `4 px`.
- Middle ground: `8 px`.
- Subject: `12 px`.
- Foreground: `18 px`.
- Camera target offset: at most `0.10` world units.
- Damping: `0.10–0.16`.

Scroll:

- Foreground moves fastest.
- Graphic UI remains anchored.
- Relative depth must reverse coherently.
- Velocity exaggeration lasts under `280 ms`.
- No layer should continue drifting after the frame hold.

### Post-effects budget

Allowed:

1. Primary: stencil-window reveal.
2. Supporting: restrained scene-specific depth or haze.
3. Ambient: paper grain.

Limits:

- Bloom intensity: `≤ 0.35`.
- Full-screen blur: `≤ 6 px`, transitions only.
- Chromatic offset: `≤ 1.2 px`, Multiverse only.
- Vignette: `≤ 0.16`, inside dark tableaux only.
- Heat distortion: `≤ 3 px`, Origin only.
- Simultaneous full-screen effects: maximum two.
- No permanent cursor trail.
- No lens flare pack.
- No glitch effect on every scene change.

## 14. Hold-to-reveal interaction specification

### Interaction state machine

```ts
type InteractionMode =
  | "idle"
  | "discoverable"
  | "scrolling"
  | "armingHold"
  | "holding"
  | "releasing"
  | "settling"
  | "routeFocus"
  | "menuOpen";
```

| State | Trigger | Required response | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idle | No input for `2.8 s` | Continuity Line gives one subtle demonstration pulse | Pointer, key, touch, scroll |
| Discoverable | Pointer enters frame or cue gains focus | Border opacity +12%; cue becomes explicit | Leave or engage |
| Scrolling | Progress velocity above threshold | Scene follows progress; hold cue quiets | Velocity settles |
| Arming hold | Pointer/touch down | `180 ms` desktop / `260 ms` touch progress ring | Release, movement, hold threshold |
| Holding | Threshold reached or Space down | `holdTarget=1`; second layer reveals | Release, Escape |
| Releasing | Hold ends | Second layer reverses in `420–560 ms` | Automatic |
| Settling | Input stops | Frame and camera reach canonical values | New input |
| Route focus | Final route focused | Preview and outcome appear | Focus leaves, activation |
| Menu open | Index activated | World time scale `0.08`; focus trapped in menu | Close, Escape, navigation |

### Reveal choreography

From `holdCurrent 0 → 1`:

```text
0.00–0.12  Slow tableau motion; emphasize hold cue
0.12–0.30  Primary copy compresses or repositions
0.20–0.56  Frame geometry begins reconfiguration
0.32–0.78  Hidden scene reveals through stencil
0.52–0.86  Continuity Line becomes causal diagram
0.68–0.94  Annotations enter
0.90–1.00  Settle and allow focused exploration
```

On release, reverse the same physical logic but allow annotations to leave `80 ms` earlier.

### Hold rules

- Holding slows scene time but does not freeze essential animation abruptly.
- Holding does not change global scroll position.
- Releasing never navigates.
- A tap shorter than the threshold does not flash the hidden layer.
- Touch movement above `12 px` cancels the hold.
- Pointer cancellation and window blur restore Story.
- The control has a visible progress state.
- The visitor can toggle the Connections state without physically holding.
- The state is described to assistive technology.
- Spoiler mode governs hidden information before it is rendered.

### Annotation behavior

- Maximum four visible annotations.
- Each annotation points to a real subject, object, or frame region.
- Leader lines use orthogonal or one-bend paths.
- Labels never overlap.
- Minimum desktop label size: `11 px`.
- Minimum mobile label size: `12 px`.
- Label placement is authored per scene.
- If placement cannot remain clear, use a numbered ledger instead.

### Single progress loop

Scroll updates only `target`.

```ts
const clamp01 = (v: number) => Math.max(0, Math.min(1, v));

const damp = (current: number, target: number, lambda: number, dt: number) =>
  THREE.MathUtils.lerp(current, target, 1 - Math.exp(-lambda * dt));

target = clamp01(scrollY / Math.max(1, scrollHeight - innerHeight));

current = damp(current, target, holding ? 2.2 : 8.5, Math.min(dt, 1 / 20));
velocity = damp(
  velocity,
  (current - previous) / Math.max(dt, 1 / 120),
  10,
  dt
);
holdCurrent = damp(holdCurrent, holdTarget, holdTarget ? 9.0 : 7.0, dt);
previous = current;
```

Helpers:

```ts
const range = (p: number, start: number, end: number) =>
  clamp01((p - start) / (end - start));

const smooth = (a: number, b: number, value: number) => {
  const t = range(value, a, b);
  return t * t * (3 - 2 * t);
};

const sceneLocal = (p: number, [start, end]: [number, number]) =>
  range(p, start, end);
```

Rules:

- One renderer.
- One primary frame loop.
- One resize owner.
- One normalized scroll source.
- React state must not update every frame for transforms.
- Frame geometry, mask geometry, and annotations derive from the same config.
- GSAP may pin and sequence DOM, but must not independently own the same camera or frame values.
- Seed procedural placement once.
- Clamp delta time after tab restoration.
- Reverse scroll must be deterministic.

- Use authored non-overlapping masks where possible.

- Keep pass count low.

## 31. Performance and adaptive quality

Targets:

- Meaningful server-rendered frame within `0.8 s` on a normal connection.
- Opening fallback immediately visible.
- Interactive desktop target: stable `55–60 FPS`.
- Recent mobile target: stable `30–60 FPS`.
- No task above `100 ms` during normal interaction.
- DPR cap: `2.0` desktop, `1.5` mobile.
- Pause rendering when hidden.
- Dispose every texture, geometry, material, render target, composer, and listener.

Quality tiers:

```ts
type QualityTier = {
  dpr: number;
  subjectLod: 0 | 1 | 2;
  connectionSceneMode: "realtime" | "plate";
  maxTexture: 1024 | 2048;
  postProcessing: "none" | "reduced" | "full";
  environmentLayers: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4;
};
```

Suggested:

| Tier | DPR | Subject | Hidden layer | Textures | Effects |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---|
| High | `1.5–2.0` | High GLB | Realtime | `2048` | Full restrained |
| Medium | `1.25–1.5` | Medium GLB | Realtime/plate | `1024–2048` | Reduced |
| Low | `1.0–1.25` | Low GLB/plate | Plate | `1024` | None/reduced |

Runtime governor:

- Sample moving average frame time.
- If above `24 ms` for `2.5 s`, reduce one quality step.
- Do not raise quality during active scroll.
- Require `8 s` stable idle before raising.
- Never change subject asset during a visible hold.

Degradation order:

1. Remove low-value particles.
2. Reduce foreground layers.
3. Reduce hidden-scene resolution.
4. Disable depth of field.
5. Disable bloom.
6. Lower texture size.
7. Switch Connections scene to an authored plate.
8. Switch entire tableau to layered 2.5D.
9. Preserve frame geometry, Continuity Line, typography, Story/Connections content, and interaction.

Context loss:

- Detect `webglcontextlost`.
- Prevent uncontrolled reload loops.
- Display current fallback.
- Attempt one clean renderer restoration.
- If restoration fails, continue in the CSS/plate mode.

Fallback:

- Static or layered chapter frames.
- CSS `clip-path` for hidden windows.
- Semantic copy and annotations.
- Complete archive routes.
- Never an empty black Canvas.

- Keep one frame.
- Keep the Continuity Line.

```html
<section aria-labelledby="origin-title">
```
